What I broke and fixed while auditing this

Stated first, because it is the fairest measure of how much of this is verified rather than asserted.

I shipped two regressions this session and only caught them by re-runningProcess
The coverage lane went out with "Sector 75, Noida" failing an exact match against "Sector 75" — so it said we do not track a sector and then recommended that same sector one sentence later. And "best 3 BHK projects" captured "bhk", a substring of Shu·bhk·amna, so a routine sector question was answered as a question about a builder nobody named. Pass rate went 96% → 80% → 98%. Both now have regression tests. The lesson is not that I fixed them; it is that a mechanical grader scored the broken version at 80% and I would have shipped it on that number alone.
I put the output sanitiser in the wrong place firstProcess
Emoji stripping went into the fallback chain. Emoji kept appearing, because the answer that carried them came through a topic handler writing to send directly. Same mistake, same shape, twice: the guarantee has to sit on the one function every token passes through, not on the path you happened to be looking at. Both the sanitiser and the payload strip now live there. 0 of 50 answers carry emoji or a competitor name.

Found by load-testing, not by reading

97% of every response was internal scoring debrisFixed
A search response was 122KB. Of that, 116KB was one properties event, and 51% of every project object inside it was _multidimensional_rank and friends — the ranker explaining itself, read by nothing on either side of the wire. Now stripped at the send choke point: 122KB → 81KB. On Indian mobile data, for 200 users, several times a session, that is real money and real render time you were spending on debug output.
One unhandled rejection would have killed the serverFixed
There was no unhandledRejection or uncaughtException handler anywhere. Node terminates the process on an unhandled rejection by default, and this codebase deliberately fires promises without awaiting them — recordUsage, the Redis cache write, PostHog tracking. Each swallows its own errors, but one slipping through takes the process down and every in-flight conversation with it. Rejections are now logged and survived; an uncaught exception logs and routes into the existing graceful shutdown rather than pretending the process is healthy.
Eight concurrent users take 25–31 seconds eachOpen · biggest unknown
Solo p50 is 3.6s. At eight concurrent it is 25–31s, and a second run of the same test timed out past 90 seconds entirely. That is the free-tier key rate-limiting, so it should improve on the paid account — but I cannot tell you by how much, because I have never run this on a funded key. Every latency number in every audit I have given you was measured single-threaded. For a 200-user beta this is the single largest unvalidated risk, and it is a twenty-five minute test the day you top up.

The roast

Specific, and mine to own where it is mine.

A third of your test suite is a lie of omission. npm test reports 2,297 tests. 1,522 pass. 774 are todo — empty placeholders that will never fail. Marking them todo instead of a green assert(true) was the right call and someone made it deliberately, but the honest reading is that you have roughly two-thirds of a safety net and it is concentrated in exactly the places I have been working. Nobody has written the tests for the parts nobody has touched.

Your quality gate cannot see wrongness. The harness I built scores shape: is it long enough, did it deflect, did it decline an out-of-scope question. It scored a four-sector brief that compared the two workplaces instead as a pass. It scored the 80% regression as 80% rather than as broken. Every quality number in this document that did not come from me reading answers one by one should be read as "well-formed", not "correct".

Nobody has clicked this in a browser in four weeks. That has been in three consecutive audits. Every mobile change, the filter dock, the card shelf, the chips I built today — none of it has been touched by a human on a phone. For a beta whose entire purpose is watching real users, shipping an unclicked interface is the highest-variance thing on this list.

Your rate limit is per-IP, not per-user. 100 requests a minute keyed on global:${ip}. Two beta users behind the same office NAT or the same mobile carrier gateway share that budget. At 50–200 users this is survivable; it is also the kind of thing that produces three confused bug reports you cannot reproduce.

It was not — I was reading the wrong table, and Project.sector covered 61 all along. That correction was worth more than most of the fixes, and it only happened because I checked a number I had already published.

The gap nobody has named yet: you cannot watch the beta

You said the point of this beta is to see what users do, get feedback, and validate the product. That is the one requirement nothing in this codebase currently satisfies well.

What exists: PostHog is wired and no-ops when unconfigured, ai_usage_events records every model call with a session id, ChatMessage holds transcripts, and CallbackRequest captures leads with a tier and a score.

What does not: there is no query that answers "what happened in the beta". Which questions did users actually ask. Where did they stop. How many reached a lead. Which answers were followed by silence — the strongest negative signal you can get and the one nobody logs. The data is being written; nothing reads it back.

This is a half-day of work and it is the difference between a beta and 200 wasted conversations. One script over ChatMessage and ai_usage_events: top queries, drop-off point per session, turns-to-lead, cost per lead, and every turn whose answer contained "not recorded" — the coverage gaps your real users find, which is worth more than the 321 queries I have been grading against.

What I would tell an investor, and what I would not

Show: two-sector comparison, a budget brief, a payment plan, a cost sheet, one named project. Every one is backed by a table rendered from your own database — it is structurally incapable of being wrong about a number, and that is a real differentiator from a chatbot on a listings site.

Do not show: anything with four sectors, a builder you do not stock, or a rental question, until you have re-run on the paid key. All three are fixed. None is verified on the model you will actually be running.

If asked about scale: the true answer is stronger than a bluff. You know your unit cost to four decimal places, you have a hard daily spend cap that fails closed, and you know your self-host break-even is around 4,750 chats a day. Founders who can say that are rarer than founders with a working demo.

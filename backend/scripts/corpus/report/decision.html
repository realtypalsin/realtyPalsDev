The short version. Your instinct — "we're burning tokens, let's take control" — is the right instinct pointed at the wrong target. The token bill is not what is stopping you from going to production. I read all fifty of your top queries one by one. Not one of the problems I found would be fixed by changing which AI model you use. Every single one was a wiring problem, a data gap, or a self-contradicting instruction. A different engine does not fix a car with the wrong address in the satnav.

First, the thing you actually asked

In plain terms, before any numbers.

"Fine-tuning" means taking a general AI model and training it further on thousands of your own examples, so it learns your style and your domain. "Self-hosting" means renting a computer with a graphics card and running that model yourself, instead of paying Google per question.

People reach for this when the AI keeps getting their domain wrong. That is not your situation. Your AI already writes genuinely good real-estate advice — I read it. When it fails, it fails because we handed it the wrong information, or asked it the wrong question, or told it two contradictory things in the same breath.

Here is the clearest example I found. A buyer wrote:

"I have ₹1.25 crore. I work near Sector 62, my wife works near Sector 135, we have one child, we need a 3 BHK ... Compare Sector 75, Sector 78, Sector 137 and Sector 150."

Our system replied with a careful, well-written comparison of Sector 62 against Sector 135 — the two places they work — and told them Sector 135 has no inventory. The four sectors they actually asked about were never looked at.

The cause: a piece of our code grabs the first two sector numbers it sees anywhere in the message. It is one line. It has nothing to do with the AI model. Fine-tuning a new model would have produced the same wrong answer, more fluently.

The GPU figure is a floor — the rental cost of the machine, nothing else. It does not include your time keeping it alive, the model getting stuck at 3am, or a second machine so a restart does not take the product down. Realistically add 50–100%.

One correction to the plan as written. You mentioned hosting on Render. Render does not offer GPUs at all — it runs web servers and databases, not AI models. So that specific plan cannot be executed as described. You would need a different provider entirely, which is a new vendor, a new bill, and a new thing that can break.

There is one number here I will not dress up: you burnt ₹1,000 in a day and it looked like ₹47 on the dashboard. That was real and it was my finding. But the cause was not the model — nine of ten places in our code that called the AI never recorded what they spent, and the price list in our code had Google's rates entered ten times too low. Both are now fixed, and there is a hard daily cap that stops the bill dead. Self-hosting would have "solved" that by replacing a variable bill with a fixed one you pay whether anyone visits or not.

What the fine-tuning would actually require

The part that usually goes unmentioned until month three.

Training data you do not haveBlocker
Fine-tuning needs thousands of examples of a question and the ideal answer. You have 321 questions and zero written ideal answers. Producing 2,000 good ones is weeks of expert real-estate writing — and if the answers are merely okay, you have trained a model to be permanently okay.
You would lose the toolsBlocker
Our chat calls real functions — EMI, stamp duty, GST, floor plans, RERA lookups. Small open models are markedly worse at deciding when to call a function and with what. That was already broken once this month for a different reason and every calculation silently fell back to guesswork. Rebuilding on a weaker foundation invites it back.
You would lose the free discount you are already gettingCost
Google currently serves 78% of every prompt from its cache at a tenth of the price, because our instructions are identical each time. That discount is invisible, automatic, and worth roughly 70% of the input side of the bill. It does not exist on your own server — there you pay for the GPU whether it is thinking or idle.
The failure mode changes shapeRisk
Today a bad day means a slow answer or a bill. With your own server, a bad day means the product is down, at 2am, and the person who fixes it is you. Before an investor demo that is a materially different kind of risk.

Where the two documents are right — and already built

I am not dismissing them. The architecture they describe is correct, and most of it now exists.

propFyndrAI.md makes exactly the right call in its first line: do not build your own LLM. Its core idea — the AI should be a presentation layer over a deterministic decision package, not the thing making the property decision — is the correct architecture, and it is the direction this codebase has been moving all week. Where I part company is only the last step: it suggests self-hosting as the destination. At your traffic, that step costs money and buys risk.

chatGPT.md is the more immediately useful of the two, and its central claim is one I verified independently before reading it: the AI should not be drawing your tables. I measured it — 54% of our answers contained a table, and tables were 53% of everything the AI wrote. Since output is about two-thirds of the bill, the AI drawing tables was roughly a third of your entire AI cost, mostly copying back data we had just sent it.

The idea from those files I have not built, and would

chatGPT.md calls it "IDs instead of data", and it is right that this is the biggest remaining saving. Today, when someone searches, we send the AI the full record of every matching project — name, builder, price, area, amenities, possession, and about 150 other fields — and ask it to pick and explain. That is roughly 7,000 words of property data per question, and we pay for every word.

The alternative: send a compact summary, let the AI reply with just the ID numbers it chose plus its reasoning, and have our own code look those IDs back up and render them. The AI becomes a judge, not a filing cabinet.

My honest estimate: it would cut the remaining bill by roughly half again. I am flagging it as an estimate rather than a measurement — unlike the numbers above, I have not run it. It is also the single largest change left, and I would not start it in the week before an investor demo.

What is actually stopping production, in order

None of these is the AI model. This is the honest list.

  1. Your AI can be confidently wrong on the hardest questions
    Three of the fifty answers I read were wrong in ways our automated scoring marked as passes — the four-sector question above, a rent table where we hold one rent figure and invented the rest, and five answers carrying emoji because the instructions contradicted themselves. All three are now fixed. None has been re-tested.
    fixed, unverified · one test run confirms
  2. You cover 14 sectors. Buyers ask about roughly 30.
    This is the big one and it is not a software problem. When someone asks about Sector 135, we correctly say we have nothing — which is honest and useless. No model, no prompt, and no amount of fine-tuning creates data you do not have. If an investor asks about a sector at random, there is a real chance we have nothing to show.
    data work · the largest lift on quality that exists
  3. Nothing has been tested on your paid account
    Your main Google key is out of credit. Everything measured this week ran on the free backup key, which uses a smaller model. The product an investor sees will be running on something I have not measured.
    ₹500 top-up · then 25 minutes to re-run
  4. An answer takes about 10 seconds
    Your own target document says under 3. Ten seconds is survivable in a demo if you talk over it; it is not survivable in a product. Fixing it trades against answer quality, so it is a decision, not a bug.
    product decision
  5. Nobody has clicked the interface on a phone
    Outstanding for three weeks now. Every mobile change is untested in a real browser.
    1 hour

Demo what is strong. Two-sector comparisons, budget briefs, payment plans and cost sheets all read like a competent human advisor and are backed by real data rendered from your database — they cannot be wrong about a number. That is a genuinely impressive demo and it is honest.

Avoid, for now: four-sector questions, builders you do not stock (Godrej, Sarthi), and anything about renting. Those are fixed but unverified, and a demo is the wrong place to find out.

If an investor asks "why not build your own AI?" — the answer that makes you look strongest is the true one: "We looked at it. Our break-even is around 5,000 conversations a day. Below that it costs more and adds an outage risk we do not need. We spend ₹0.18 a chat with a hard daily cap, and our data and our decision logic — the parts that are actually ours — already live in our own code. We'll revisit hosting when volume justifies it." That is a founder who has done the arithmetic. Building it now, at your volume, reads as the opposite.

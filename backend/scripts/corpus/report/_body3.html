The 96% is real but it is not the answer to your question. The grader checks for deflections, empty replies, truncation and out-of-scope answers that should have been declined. What reading the answers found

Each of these passed the mechanical grade. Each is a defect a buyer would notice.

The hardest query compared the wrong two sectors Fixed
Asked: "I have ₹1.25 crore. I work near Sector 62, my wife works near Sector 135, we have one child … Compare Sector 75, Sector 78, Sector 137 and Sector 150." The answer compared Sector 62 against Sector 135 — the two workplaces — and reported that Sector 135 has no inventory. The four sectors actually asked about were never looked at. sectorComparison takes sectorMatches[0] and [1], the first two sector numbers anywhere in the message. Now it only claims a turn with exactly two; three or more is a brief and goes to the advisory path that sees the whole intent.
A rent table built almost entirely from data we do not hold Fixed
"rental properties in noida" opened honestly — "I can only provide rental benchmarks for the micro-markets we cover" — and then drew a grid of 2 BHK and 3 BHK monthly ranges across four micro-markets. We hold one rent field, avg_rent_3bhk_monthly, on sector rows. Every other cell was invented, and the honest opener made it read as verified. The scope rule now permits a single benchmark inline in a sentence and forbids a rent table outright.
The prompt contradicted itself on emoji, and the emoji won Fixed
One section reads "NO EMOJI, ANYWHERE. Not in headings, not in table cells… An advisor writing about a two-crore decision does not decorate it." Another mandates a 🏗️ Coverage Status header. Five of fifty answers carried emoji. When a prompt contradicts itself the model picks one, and it picked the concrete instruction over the principle.
A brand we do not stock is answered with city-wide data Open
"godrej properties in noida" returned the micro-market rate table and never mentioned Godrej. "sarthi properties in noida" said "I can't reach our builder database right now" — which reads as a transient outage when the truth is we hold no Sarthi projects. Honest-sounding, and misleading in a different direction.

The pattern is clear and it is not what a pass rate shows. The middle of the distribution is strong: two-way comparisons, budget briefs, statutory and process questions all read like a competent advisor. The extremes are where it breaks — the most constrained briefs get the wrong framing, and the barest head terms get generic orientation. Those extremes are exactly what a demo audience reaches for.

Your two ideas, and what they are worth

Chips that adapt to the answer just givenHighest value left
The chip prompt already says "pick up a thread that answer opened" — but the chips are generated by a second model call that re-reads the conversation, which is both the expensive way and a lossy one. The answer we just rendered is structured: we know which projects were in the table, which sectors, whether a payment plan was shown. Chips can be derived from that structure in code — "Payment plans for ACE Parkway", "Why Sector 150 over 128", "Cost sheet for this project" — with no model call at all. That removes the last per-turn LLM call, makes chips deterministic, and makes them more relevant than a model re-reading its own output. This is the single best remaining item and it is not large.
Database first, web second, never a third thingPartly there
groundedAnswer.ts already does this: our tables first, live web second, and it refuses when it has neither. Every claim must carry its source in parentheses, and web results are marked untrusted so instructions inside them are ignored. What is not yet enforced is the third case — the model answering from its own memory when both are empty. That is what produced the rent table and the invented sector metrics. The renderers close it for anything tabular; prose is still on trust.

The verdict

Ready for a soft launch. Ready for a demo only with a chosen script.

What is genuinely production-grade: the money cannot run away again — every call is metered, the day is capped, and the gate fails closed. Tables come from the database and cannot invent a column. The provider chain fails over for real reasons and logs why. 100% of projects carry payment plans, cost sheets and RERA.

What is not: the system can still be confidently wrong on the hardest questions, and I only know that because I read the answers rather than trusting the score. Two of the three defects I found are fixed and unmeasured; the third — brand and coverage gaps — is a data problem.

If you demo it, drive it through two-way comparisons, budget briefs, payment plans and cost sheets. Those read well and are backed by rendered data. Avoid four-sector briefs and named builders we do not stock until the reruns are in.

The honest order: top up the key → re-run --top50 → add a judge pass → fill sector coverage. The first two are an afternoon and turn every projection in this document into a fact.

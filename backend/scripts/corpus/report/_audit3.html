Why my first number was wrong

Two defects, both mine, both introduced this morning.

In plain English: think of the test harness as a marker grading exam papers. I gave it a new subject to mark, but forgot to give it a pass mark for that subject. With no pass mark, every paper passed — including a blank one. That's how a 6-character answer scored the same as a full one.

The length check never ranFixed
The grader holds a minimum answer length per category. My long-tail set introduced twelve new category names — price, rera, brochure — none of which were in that table. Looking up a missing key returns undefined, and length < undefined is always false in JavaScript. So the length gate silently stopped running for all 120. A 65-character answer passed a floor that never executed. It now fails closed with a default of 120 characters.
An outage was scored as a successFixed
30 of 120 turns returned "Our AI services are currently experiencing high traffic or are out of service." That message is 190 characters of polite prose, so it cleared every length check and was graded pass. A test suite that scores an outage as a win is measuring the wrong thing. There is now an unavailable grade.
Chips were never recorded at allFixed
You asked me to rate the chips. The harness captured only the prose — no chips, no property cards — so there was nothing to rate. It now records both, which is how the chip findings further down exist.

The single biggest problem: 25% of turns got no answer at all

In plain English: we rent our AI from Google. That rental has a speed limit. Three users at once, for ten minutes, and we hit it — Google starts refusing. We're supposed to fall back to a different supplier when that happens, and the fallback did not cover us. One in four people got an error page instead of an answer.

Measured, from the server log of the run: 57 refusals from Gemini, 3 from Mistral, 1 from Groq, and 616 fallback events. The chain rolled over as designed and still ran dry, because Gemini is doing nearly all the work and the paid key is rate-limited.

The same four generic chips appear on 47 of 120 turns — after a question about a brochure, about an address, about a construction update. They are not responses to anything. And on 39 turns the only chip offered was "I need a 3 BHK", which is a filter, not a next step, and is nonsense after "what is the RERA number".

The axis-based chips I built today — "What competes with Ace Hanei?" / "Full cost of Ace Hanei" / "Is Ace Hanei RERA clean?" — are the good ones, and they fired 6 times out of 120. They are being overridden by hardcoded chip sets inside the individual topic handlers, which run earlier and win.

In plain English: I rebuilt the buttons under each answer so they'd be about what you just asked. But there are older, hardcoded buttons buried in fourteen separate places in the code, and those fire first. My new ones only get a turn when none of the old ones do. Fixing this means deleting the hardcoded sets, not adding more logic.

The honest bottom line

The good answers are genuinely good. The payment schedule, the floor plans, the honest handling of a rental question — those are advisor-grade and no listings portal does them.

But 58% is the real figure, and the gap is not subtle: a quarter of turns returned nothing at all because we outran our AI quota at three concurrent users, and another quarter answered like a database rather than an advisor.

The single most useful thing I did today was not a fix — it was discovering that my own test harness had been reporting success for failure. Everything above depends on that having been caught.

The two fabrications found today

In plain English: two places in the system were making up numbers and presenting them as facts we had checked. Not the AI hallucinating — numbers we had typed in and forgotten about, which the AI then read out in good faith.

A price table invented inside the AI's instructionsRemoved
The system prompt carried a hardcoded market table under a heading reading "MANDATORY TABULAR BREAKDOWN — You MUST present". It was not from the database. Measured against our own unit rows the same day:
Micro-marketThe prompt assertedOur rows actually hold
Noida Expressway₹1.80–2.60 Cr₹1.25–5.80 Cr
Central Noida 7X₹1.65–2.25 Cr₹1.15–4.60 Cr
Greater Noida West₹1.25–1.85 Cr₹0.86–2.55 Cr

Every ceiling understated by more than half; every floor set too high. A buyer with ₹1 crore was told Greater Noida West starts at ₹1.25 Cr while we hold 3 BHKs from ₹0.86 Cr — we were turning away buyers we could serve. It was also the largest single source of the unbidden tables: a must-use table of invented numbers outranking the per-question rule that says one fact is a sentence.

A handover date that was a rubber stampWithheld
expected_handover_quarter holds exactly three values across 280 projects: null on 189, "Delivered" on 62, and "Q4 2026" on 29. On 23 of those 29 it contradicts the project's own possession date by up to 29 months — and both were printed in the same answer. Withheld from buyers until the values are real; possession_date already carries the answer.

And one that turned out fine. Eight more fields are dominated by a single value — legal_flag_detail reading "Clean land title. No pending legal disputes" on 91 projects looked like the same problem, and a fabricated clean title is the worst thing this product could say. It checked out: all 91 rows carry land_title_clear = true and zero litigation, and all 207 "Fully RERA & Authority Approved" rows carry a RERA number. Templated wording, real evidence underneath. Left alone.

How the searches are kept apart

You asked how I'd do this if I were designing ChatGPT. This is the model.

In plain English: a good assistant does not send every question to the AI. It first works out what kind of question it is. Some it can answer straight from the database — faster, free, and impossible to get wrong. Some it should refuse. Only what is genuinely left over goes to the AI.

  1. 1 · Is this even about us?
    "cameron county 107 district court" is a Texas courthouse that shares a string with County 107 in Sector 107. We answered it with that project's swimming pool. Now declined — but only when no Noida marker appears anywhere, so "district court near Sector 62" still answers.
  2. 2 · Does the place mean exactly one place?
    "Sector 1" resolves to Sector 1 only — never 10, 100 or 107 — because matching is whole-word, not substring. "Sector 12, Greater Noida West" narrows to that city rather than widening to the region. "Sector 107" exists in two cities, so it asks which instead of merging them.
  3. 3 · Does the thing they named exist, and does it have what they asked for?
    "county 107 3 bhk price" used to answer ₹4.85 Cr. County 107 builds 4 and 5 BHK. Now it says so and shows what nearby actually offers a 3 BHK — checked before any handler answers, because handlers answer their own topic and never question the premise.
  4. 4 · Can the database answer without the AI?
    Payment schedules, cost sheets, project shortlists, sector PIN codes, builder coverage — all rendered from our own rows. Free, instant, and incapable of inventing a figure. A rendered table also cannot grow a column we have no data for.
  5. 5 · Only now, the AI — and only with our rows in hand
    The model reasons over facts we hand it. It never supplies a rate from memory: if the verified block was not injected this turn, it does not hold market rates and must say so.
  6. 6 · If anything fails, say so plainly
    No default number, no "typical" value standing in for a real one. Absent means absent, and the honest sentence is the answer.
Before"sector 10 noida extension pin code" → 201301 on one run, 201306 on the next. Neither matches our records. Nothing in the schema holds a PIN, so it was guessed.
AfterRead from the addresses we already hold: 203207 on 12 of the 15 projects there — with the 3 that disagree named, rather than averaged away.

The bottom line

62%. The foundations are sound: retrieval finds the right rows, prices match the units they came from, invented figures are out of the prompt, and four audits now stand between a data defect and a buyer.

What is left is one hard blocker and one unknown. The blocker is capacity — at three concurrent users we fail a quarter of the time, and that is a bill to pay rather than code to write. The unknown is that nobody has used this on a phone.

Everything else is data work, and it is now directed by what real users could not get answered rather than by what I guessed they would ask.

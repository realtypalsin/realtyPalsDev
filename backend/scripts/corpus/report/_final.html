What I built this session

Sector answers now come from the projects themselvesBiggest win
When no hand-written row exists, we compute the sector from its projects: how many, how many ready, the price band, the landmark names. That is 41 sectors describable instead of 14, and it needed no new data at all — the numbers were already sitting in the same database, thrown away because a different table happened to be short. Derived rows are marked as derived, and a curated row always wins where one exists, because it carries judgement a GROUP BY cannot.
Chips are built in code, with no AI call at allYour idea
Chips were a second model call that re-read the transcript to guess what its own answer had said. We do not have to guess — we rendered the answer, so we know which projects are on screen. A chip can now say "Full cost of ACE Parkway" because ACE Parkway is genuinely in the table above it. This removes the last per-turn AI call on a simple query, roughly a quarter of its cost. One rule is enforced by test: if there is nothing specific to offer, show nothing rather than "Tell me more".
We were searching the four websites we are forbidden to citeFixed
The web-search allowlist contained 99acres, MagicBricks, NoBroker and Housing.com — the exact four portals the prompt bans by name and the output guardrail flags. So we fetched facts from sources we could not attribute, leaving the model two bad options: name the source and trip the guardrail, or state the figure unattributed as though it were ours. The second is what a scraped listing average looks like when it reaches a buyer, and it is indistinguishable from invention. The list is now the regulator, the three development authorities, CREDAI and financial press — sources a buyer can check and we can name. A test fails if a banned domain ever returns.

Tables: only when they earn it

You asked for tables everywhere and then, correctly, for tables only when needed. Both are now true.

Six tables are drawn by our own code from database rows: project shortlist, micro-market, derived sector, sector-vs-sector, payment plans, cost sheet. None of them can invent a column, put an emoji in a cell, or state a figure we do not hold — a missing value prints "Not recorded".

And they are suppressed where they would be noise. A table is not drawn when property cards are already showing the same five columns; a question about one project gets prose; a bare head term gets two sentences. When the model draws one anyway — measured at 11 times in 50 — a filter strips it from the stream, so the buyer never sees the same figures twice.

Measured: tables were 53% of everything the AI wrote, and output is about two-thirds of the bill. That made the AI drawing tables roughly a third of your entire AI cost, mostly copying back data we had just sent it.

On building your own AI

Holding until 5,000 chats a day is the right call, and the arithmetic holds: your break-even is around 4,750 chats/day, you spend roughly ₹0.18 per chat today, and a GPU costs about ₹25,000/month whether anyone visits or not. Below that line self-hosting costs more and adds an outage risk you do not need before a demo.

On the phone-sized model you mentioned — those are real, and genuinely impressive engineering. But a model small enough to run on a handset is not the model that writes a defensible ₹1.5-crore recommendation. Where it could earn its place is the cheap mechanical work: classifying what a question is about, extracting a budget from a sentence. Worth revisiting once volume justifies the infrastructure; not worth it as a way to save ₹5,000 a month today.

There is one idea from chatGPT.md I have deliberately not built: sending the AI project IDs instead of full records, and looking them up ourselves. My estimate is it halves the remaining bill again. It is also the largest change left, and the week before an investor demo is the wrong week to start it.
